Where the model looked

Heat on a corner marker or outside the lungs means the model found a shortcut. But heat on the lungs does not clear it — scanner and exposure signature is present inside the lung fields too, so a model reading provenance rather than disease still produces a plausible-looking map.

Why to distrust it

  • The classes are separable by source, not just by disease. In the dataset this was trained on, every COVID-19 image came from BIMCV, Eurorad, SIRM or GitHub, and every Normal and Pneumonia image came from Kaggle. No overlap. A model can score highly by learning which repository an image came from.
  • The label is not the disease. Labels came from whoever assembled the dataset, by varying and mostly undocumented criteria.
  • The class balance is not prevalence. Nothing here estimates how likely any given patient is to have anything.
  • Three classes is not every finding. The check above catches images that are not chest X-rays. It does not catch a real chest X-ray showing something this model has no class for — tested on lung opacity films, most were accepted and then called NORMAL at high confidence. "NORMAL" here means "not the other two", never "clear".

Any real concern about a chest X-ray belongs with a qualified radiologist or physician.
